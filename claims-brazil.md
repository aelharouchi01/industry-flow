# Brazil: claims, sources and what not to say

A defensibility record for the Brazil map. Every claim the artifact makes is listed
with where it was checked and how far it can be pushed in front of a client.

Verified 14 August 2026. Anything dated should be re-checked before reuse.

## How to read the status column

| Status | Meaning | Safe to assert? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A** | Confirmed against a government, official or first-party source | Yes, cite it |
| **B** | Confirmed against a reputable secondary source, or a legal database rendering of a primary text | Yes, but attribute softly and expect a follow-up question |
| **C** | Not verified in this pass | No. Say "I would need to confirm that figure" |
| **REMOVED** | Was in an earlier build, could not be sourced, deleted | Never say it |

One general caution. The Planalto legislation server refused connections
throughout this verification pass, so decree article text was confirmed through a
legal database rendering and corroborated against government and press summaries.
Before a high stakes presentation, open the Planalto links below yourself and
confirm firsthand. They are the authoritative text.

### The correction that matters most

An earlier build of this map drew the science ministry as a **detached box**, on the
claim that neither science nor planning held a seat on the council, and presented
that absence as the sharpest finding on the page. **That claim is false.** Both
ministries are named in Article 3. The box is now connected.

If anyone has already seen the earlier version, correct it before they raise it.
The honest replacement finding is about specialisation, not exclusion: government
foresight reaches the council through ministries that each carry a broad brief,
while industry maintains a standing unit whose only job is looking forward.

The R$653.2 billion figure is the total for **the whole credit plan across every
funder**. An earlier build attributed it to the development bank alone, which

share. If you have circulated a version with R$653 billion against BNDES, correct
it. A Brazilian audience will know.

## 7. Ground

The Brazil map has no Ground layer. No single national land or licensing
chokepoint was identified, unlike the Emirates where an emirate level licence is
required. **Status C**: this is an absence asserted by the map, not a sourced
finding. If a client asks who permits a plant in Brazil, say you would need to
confirm the municipal and state permitting picture.

## 8. The independent critique, worth having ready

[Cambridge Industrial Innovation Policy](https://www.ciip.group.cam.ac.uk/reports-and-articles/brazil-new-industrial-policy-plan/)
published an assessment of the policy. Three sources of optimism: leading
economies have made industrial policy legitimate again, with the United States
CHIPS and Science Act and Inflation Reduction Act together reaching over one
trillion dollars; supply chain resilience and sustainability create genuine
openings for Brazilian production; and macroeconomic conditions, including an
exchange rate near five to one, favour industrial exporters.

Three cautions: missions are not a replacement for sectoral support and need
sectoral knowledge underneath them; coordination gaps between agencies mean state
capacity has to be strengthened; and the policy must be able to tolerate failure
and withdraw from cases that do not work.

The second caution maps directly onto the thin Translation layer in the diagram,
which is useful. If a client raises it, point at the map rather than argue.

## 9. The short list of things never to say

1. That the science ministry has no seat on the industrial council, or that
   government has no foresight input. Both are wrong.
2. That the development bank approved R$653 billion. That is the whole plan across
   all funders. The bank is about R$300 billion of it.
3. R$27 billion a year of CNI directed spending. Unsourced.
4. One million companies represented. Unsourced.
5. 3.3 million training places and 114,000 internships as precise figures.
6. That every mission has a 2023 baseline and a 2026 checkpoint. Only the 2033
   horizon and mission level targets are confirmed.
