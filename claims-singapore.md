# Singapore: claims, sources and what not to say

A defensibility record for the Singapore map, in the same format as
`claims-brazil.md`.

Verified 14 August 2026. **This map ages faster than Brazil's.** Singapore
restructured its economic strategy between August 2025 and May 2026, and one
consequence is that two of the agencies named on the board are being merged out of
existence. Re-check before every use.

## How to read the status column

| Status | Meaning | Safe to assert? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A** | Confirmed against a government, official or first-party source | Yes, cite it |
| **B** | Confirmed against a reputable secondary source | Yes, but attribute softly |
| **C** | Not verified in this pass | No. Say "I would need to confirm that" |
| **REMOVED** | Was in an earlier build, could not be confirmed, deleted | Never say it |

## 8. The short list of things never to say

1. That the union officer sits on both the investment board and the enterprise
   agency board. Only the investment board is confirmed.
2. That the National AI Council was created out of the review's final report. It
   came in the February Budget, three months earlier.
3. That JTC holds 86 per cent of industrial land. Say more than 80 per cent.
4. That the Skills Frameworks cover more than 130 job roles. Unconfirmed.
5. "More than 80 engagements" during the review. Unconfirmed. The 7,700
   stakeholder figure is fine.
6. Anything naming SkillsFuture Singapore or Workforce Singapore as permanent
   institutions. They are merging.
7. Any specific board member without first reading the 31 March 2026 MTI board
   changes release.

## 9. The thing that actually got stronger

The tripartite thesis, which is the whole point of the Singapore map, survived
verification and came out better than it went in. Two facts now carry it, both
sourced:

**Desmond Choo** co-chairs an Economic Strategy Review committee while being at
once Minister of State for Defence and deputy secretary-general of the union
congress. **Caryn Lim Tze Ching** sits on the board of the investment board while
being an assistant secretary-general of the union congress and chief executive of
its employment institute.

In neither case is the union consulted from outside. In both cases the same person
holds both hats. That is a sharper way to put it than "government, business and
unions sit inside the same bodies", and it is defensible with two primary sources.
